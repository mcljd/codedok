Coded vessel categories explained: Cat 0 to Cat 6

UK coded vessels are classified into seven Categories — Cat 0 through Cat 6 — based on how far from a safe haven they can operate. Lower numbers = further offshore = more rigorous equipment, stability and crew requirements. Here's what each Category actually means.

The big picture

Categories are an operating envelope. They don't describe what kind of boat you have — they describe how far from shore that boat is allowed to operate commercially. A Category 2 certificate means your vessel can operate up to 60 miles from a safe haven. Step outside that envelope and you're operating outside your certification.

The Categories run from Cat 0 (unrestricted) down to Cat 6 (within 3 miles of land, in favourable weather and daylight). They cover operation at sea — inland waterways are regulated separately.

Each Category, plain English

CAT 0

Unrestricted service

Operating area: No distance limit.

Master (minimum): Yachtmaster Ocean, or an STCW Master certificate for unlimited area.

Key equipment: Liferaft, two EPIRBs, two SARTs, 12 parachute + 6 red hand flares + 2 buoyant smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category A medical stores.

CAT 1

Up to 150 miles from a safe haven

Operating area: Up to 150 miles from a safe haven.

Master (minimum): Yachtmaster Offshore or higher; a second person holding at least Yachtmaster Coastal.

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB, two SARTs, 6 parachute + 6 red hand flares + 2 buoyant smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category B medical stores.

CAT 2

Up to 60 miles from a safe haven

Operating area: Up to 60 miles from a safe haven.

Master (minimum): Yachtmaster Offshore or higher.

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB, 4 parachute + 6 red hand flares + 2 smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category C medical stores.

CAT 3

Up to 20 miles from a safe haven

Operating area: Up to 20 miles from a safe haven.

Master (minimum): Yachtmaster Coastal, Boatmasters’ Licence, Powerboat Advanced, or higher.

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB, 4 parachute + 6 red hand flares + 2 smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category C medical stores.

CAT 4

Up to 20 miles from a safe haven, favourable weather, daylight

Operating area: Up to 20 miles from a safe haven, in favourable weather and in daylight.

Master (minimum): As Category 3, or Day Skipper Theory & Practical (daylight only).

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB, 2 red hand flares + 2 smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category C medical stores.

CAT 5

Within 3 miles of land, favourable weather

Operating area: Within 3 miles of land and no more than 3 miles from the point of departure (or the seaward boundary of categorised waters), in favourable weather.

Master (minimum): Yachtmaster Coastal, Boatmasters’ Licence, Powerboat Advanced, or higher.

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB recommended (or one carried), 4 parachute + 6 red hand flares + 2 smoke, thermal protective aids for everyone, Category C medical stores.

CAT 6

Within 3 miles of land, favourable weather, daylight

Operating area: As Category 5, but in daylight only.

Master (minimum): Any Category 5 qualification, Day Skipper (daylight), a Local Authority Licence for the area, or Powerboat Level 2 with 12 months’ relevant experience (small open boats/RIBs under 10 m).

Key equipment: Liferaft, EPIRB recommended, 2 red hand flares + 2 smoke, Category C medical stores.

Summary of SPV Code §3.10.1 (areas), Table A5.1 (Master qualifications), Table 14.1.2 (life-saving appliances) and §23.1.1 (medical stores). Every category also needs lifejackets for everyone on board, lifebuoys, VHF and more — always check the full Code and with your Certifying Authority. The Workboat Code uses the same area categories.

How to pick the right Category for your operation

The temptation is to apply for the highest Category your vessel could possibly meet — gives maximum operating flexibility. The cost: more equipment, more rigorous crew tickets, higher insurance, more frequent inspection. Pragmatic charter operators size their Category to their actual operating area, with a small buffer.

  • If 95% of your charters are inside 10 nm: Cat 3 or Cat 4.
  • If you genuinely cross to the Mull of Kintyre or out to Rathlin: Cat 2.
  • If you do offshore deliveries or sail training that crosses the Irish Sea: Cat 1.
  • If you stay within 3 miles of land and of where you set off: Cat 5 (or Cat 6 for daylight-only trips).

Categories vs jurisdictions vs Codes

These three terms get muddled. Here's the difference:

  • Code = which rulebook applies (MCA SPV Code 2025, Workboat Code Ed.3, Irish PBL)
  • Category = the operating envelope within a UK Code (Cat 0–6)
  • Jurisdiction = which national authority signs off (UK MCA, Irish MSO)

A vessel can be UK MCA Cat 2 under SPV Code 2025 and Irish PBL Class P4 simultaneously. Two certificates, two annual surveys, one vessel. More on Irish PBL Classes here.

What changed under the SPV Code 2025

The Category system itself didn't change — Cat 0 to Cat 6 stays, with the same definitions in the Workboat Code. What changed under the new Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code 2025:

  • A liferaft in every category, and thermal protective aids for everyone in Cat 0–5
  • A Safety Management System for every vessel, due by 12 December 2028
  • Stability booklets triggered by Cat 0/1, 16+ persons, heavy activity equipment or a lifting device — not by length
  • Clearer Master qualifications per category (e.g. Day Skipper only in Cat 4 and 6, daylight)
