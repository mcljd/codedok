What changes in the MCA Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code 2025 — operator's checklist

The new MCA Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code (2nd edition) came into force on 12 December 2025. Here are the changes skippers actually need to act on — plus a checklist to walk into your next survey clean.

What's actually changing

The Code is given legal effect by the Merchant Shipping (Vessels in Commercial Use for Sport or Pleasure) Regulations 2025. It replaces the Yellow Code, Blue Code, Red Code and the IPV Code, and ends the use of MGN 280 for sport or pleasure vessels (SPV Code §1.8). It covers small commercial vessels in use for sport or pleasure that carry no more than 12 passengers and no cargo (§1.1).

If you operate a chartered yacht, motor cruiser, RIB trip boat, sailing school or similar in commercial use for sport or pleasure, the SPV Code applies to you. If you operate a workboat, pilot boat, dive support or wind-farm support vessel, you stay under Workboat Code Edition 3 — see Workboat Code Edition 3 vs SPV Code — which applies?.

1. When it applies to you

New vessels (keel laid on or after 12 December 2025) must meet the Code in full. Existing coded vessels must meet it by their next renewal examination or 12 December 2028 (three years after the Code came into force), whichever is later (Appendix 12 §3). Some sections give existing vessels their own allowances — for example fuel tanks and piping.

2. A Safety Management System for every vessel

This is the change most likely to catch operators out. All vessels within the scope of the Code — not just skippered charter, and including owners/operators of bareboat charter vessels — must implement a Safety Management System that is proportionate to the size and complexity of the vessel and the business, covering both shore-side and marine aspects. It must be in place within three years of the Code coming into force (by 12 December 2028), and the Certifying Authority reviews it at examinations (§30.1–30.2).

Appendix 7 sets out the areas an SMS must address:

  1. General — vessel and company
  2. Health, safety and environmental protection policy
  3. Risk assessments for safe working for all operations
  4. Roles and responsibilities
  5. Training and qualifications of personnel
  6. Vessel standard operating procedures
  7. Vessel emergency procedures
  8. Reporting of accidents
  9. Maintenance of the vessel and equipment
  10. Cyber security measures
  11. Review and continuous improvement
  12. Single-handed operation (only where the Certifying Authority permits it)

You don't need a 100-page document — you need those areas covered in a way that fits your operation. CodedOK's SMS builder follows these Appendix 7 headings and exports a document you can send to your Certifying Authority.

3. Stability — the trigger isn't length

An approved Stability Information Booklet is required for vessels in Area Category 0 or 1, carrying 16 or more persons, carrying activity equipment over 1,000 kg (or with a free-surface effect), or fitted with a lifting device (§12.1.1.3). Other vessels use the simplified stability assessment (heel test) or ISO 12217 categorisation (§12A). If you modify the vessel, agree it with your Certifying Authority first — a significant change can mean a new assessment.

4. Equipment carriage in one set of tables

Life-saving appliances are set out in Table 14.1.2 and radio in Section 17. Points worth checking:

  • Liferaft in every area category.
  • Lifejackets for 100% of persons on board; thermal protective aids for everyone in Category 0–5.
  • EPIRB: two in Category 0, one in Category 1–4; recommended in Category 5 and 6.
  • Flares: quantities depend on category (e.g. Category 0: 12 parachute, 6 red hand, 2 buoyant smoke).
  • Medical stores: Category A for Area Category 0, B for Category 1, C for Category 2–6 (§23.1.1).

5. Crew qualifications

Table A5.1 sets which Master's certificates are accepted in each area category. For example, Day Skipper is accepted only in Category 4 and 6 (daylight), and Powerboat Level 2 only in Category 6 with 12 months' relevant experience. RYA certificates need a commercial endorsement, and a second competent person is required on board. A fire-fighting course — and, where a Stability Information Booklet is needed, a stability course for the Master — must be done within five years (by 12 December 2030).

6. Owner duties

The Code places clear duties on owners: the annual examination carried out by the owner/managing agent for smaller (Group 1) vessels (§4.5.2), an annual airtightness test declaration for RIBs and inflatables (§4.9), agreeing modifications with the Certifying Authority before making them, and reporting incidents to the Certifying Authority (§4.8). Operator's checklist

What to do before your next renewal

  • Gather every certificate and record on file and check each against the SPV Code.
  • Work out your stability route (booklet or simplified assessment) and make sure the evidence matches the vessel as it is now.
  • Build or refresh your Safety Management System against the Appendix 7 headings.
  • Check the Master's certificate is accepted for your area category and is commercially endorsed.
  • Audit your equipment against Table 14.1.2 and Section 17 and list anything to buy or service.
  • Talk to your Certifying Authority about when your vessel moves to the new Code (next renewal or 12 December 2028, whichever is later).
